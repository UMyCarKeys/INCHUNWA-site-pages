bio: |
  Brit Reed is a multidisciplinary artist, researcher, chef, educator, and cultural storyteller whose work explores how Southeastern Indigenous identity is carried through food, the body, clothing, adornment, and visual art. Raised in Dallas, Texas, by a non-Indigenous family, Reed’s experience as an adoptee has deeply shaped her journey of reconnecting with her Choctaw ancestry, community, and cultural inheritance.

  Reed studied Native American Studies and Tribal governance at The Evergreen State College, where she examined the relationships among colonization, food insecurity, community health, cultural continuity, and Tribal sovereignty. During that time, she became an advocate for Indigenous food sovereignty, founding Food Sovereignty Is Tribal Sovereignty, an online community created to connect and uplift Indigenous cooks, food producers, and knowledge keepers. She later trained at the Seattle Culinary Academy to expand her work in the food sovereignty movement to include cooking traditional food for the public.

  Working across beadwork, digital illustration, photography, culinary arts, research, and storytelling, Reed draws inspiration from Choctaw and broader Southeastern Indigenous histories. Her work responds to the limited visibility of Southeastern Tribal peoples in contemporary art and culture, creating space for community members to see their identities, aesthetics, and continued presence reflected.

  As Founder and Communications Director of the Inchunwa Project, Reed supports the research, documentation, and responsible revitalization of Southeastern Indigenous tattoo and ancestral body-marking traditions. Through visual art, oral history, public education, podcasting, and community-centered learning, she explores tattooing, clothing, adornment, and foodways as living expressions of memory, kinship, body sovereignty, and cultural continuity. Her work considers how practices disrupted by removal, adoption, and colonialism can be reclaimed and carried forward for future generations.
