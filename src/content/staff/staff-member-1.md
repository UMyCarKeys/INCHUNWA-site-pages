bio: |
  Lisa Fruichantie (Harjo) is an activist, futurist, entrepreneur, designer, and cultural strategist whose work cultivates transformative shared spaces and builds bridges across communities. A citizen of the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma with Mvskoke ancestry, she was born in the Ute hunting territories of southern Colorado and raised across Alaska, Oklahoma, and the Four Corners region.

  During winters in Alaska, Fruichantie was immersed in Dena’ina culture and community alongside the Kenaitze people. Summers spent with her grandparents, aunts, and uncles in Oklahoma and the Southwest deepened her connection to her Southeastern Tribal identity, cultural traditions, and family histories. These experiences of movement across lands, cultures, and kinship networks continue to shape her understanding of identity as something carried, practiced, protected, and transformed across generations.

  Now based on the homelands of the Puyallup people in Tacoma, Washington, Fruichantie brings these lived connections to work honoring the sovereignty of Indigenous art, culture, bodies, and heritage. As Project Director of the Inchunwa Project, she supports the revitalization of Southeastern Indigenous identity and ancestral body-marking practices as pathways toward rematriation, cultural continuity, body sovereignty, and collective healing.
